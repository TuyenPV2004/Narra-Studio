# Why Efficient AI Can Demand More Power

One AI task can get dramatically cheaper in energy terms while the industry as a whole demands more electricity.

The International Energy Agency says energy per AI task has recently fallen by at least an order of magnitude each year. But the same report warns that video, reasoning, and agentic tasks can use hundreds or thousands of times more energy than a simple text query.

Now add adoption. IEA's central case has global data-center electricity use rising from 485 terawatt-hours in 2025 to 950 in 2030, with AI-focused facilities growing faster.

The forecast is not destiny. Berkeley Lab's U.S. model lands at 649 terawatt-hours in its reference case, but stretches from 521 to 843 across uncertainty scenarios.

That spread tells the real story. Total demand is the result of three moving variables: efficiency, how many people use AI, and how intensive their tasks become. The future depends on which one moves fastest.
