# Research packet — Run A

## Question

Why is AI increasing data-center electricity demand even as computing becomes more efficient?

## Evidence

- Global data-center electricity use is projected to rise from 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh in 2030, with AI-focused facilities growing faster. `[fact-global-demand]`
- Berkeley Lab's U.S. reference case is 649 TWh in 2030, but the modeled uncertainty range is 521–843 TWh. `[fact-us-range]`

## Interpretation

The documentary should frame AI electricity demand as an infrastructure scaling problem: rapid deployment and denser workloads can outrun grids and generation even while each unit of computation improves.

## Counterpoint and uncertainty

Both headline numbers are projections. Slower deployment, bottlenecks, and better efficiency can lower demand, so the story must show a range rather than claim an inevitable crisis.
