# AI's Electricity Bottleneck

AI can become more efficient and still use much more electricity. The reason is scale.

The International Energy Agency's central projection puts global data-center electricity consumption at 485 terawatt-hours in 2025. By 2030, it reaches 950. Electricity use from AI-focused facilities grows even faster, tripling over the same period.

That growth becomes a local infrastructure problem. Berkeley Lab's U.S. reference case reaches 649 terawatt-hours in 2030. But its wider range runs from 521 to 843, or 9.5 to 15.3 percent of national electricity use.

The range matters. These are scenarios, not a meter reading from the future. Faster chips can cut the energy needed for one task, while more users, denser racks, and more demanding applications push total consumption upward.

So the bottleneck is not one wasteful query. It is the speed mismatch between AI deployment and the grids, generation, and equipment expected to power it.
